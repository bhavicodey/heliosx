    how the data is extracted

    nothing is downloaded or stored. every time you pick a date or drop a pin, google earth engine reads satellite archives for just that area and sends back map tiles and numbers.

    1. sulfur dioxide comes from the sentinel-5p satellite. cloudy pixels (over 30% cloud) are thrown out, the 7 days up to your date are combined into a median, and the result is shown in dobson units. the readout compares it with the same area's average over the 30 days before.
    2. active fires come from your selected sensor: MODIS FIRMS (~1 km) or VIIRS VNP14A1 (a daily 1 km composite). Each satellite product has different detection characteristics, so counts are not directly comparable. The calendar shows daily detected fire pixels, not verified fires or prescribed burns.
    3. burn scars come from sentinel-2 photos. clouds, shadows and snow are masked, then each pixel's near-infrared and shortwave-infrared light is compared before the fire and now. a big drop (dnbr above 0.27) on land that was green before counts as burned, and the pixels are added up in hectares.

    alert = fire plus sulfur dioxide at 1.5 times normal or more. watch = 10+ fire pixels, or sulfur dioxide at 2 times normal. it means the two show up together, not that one caused the other.

    where this came from

    hi, i'm bhavishaya vignesh. i used to live in canada, and i lived through the wildfires in alberta and british columbia. the smoke changed the sky and the air we breathed, and it was hard on my family. i wanted to understand what was in that air and how to see it coming, and satellites turned out to see it first. i built firescout because if the data is already up there, it should feel as friendly as opening a map.

    ★
    tri-valley air quality award

    i won the tri-valley air quality award for this project and the research behind it on sulfur scout and wildfires. firescout is where that research became something anyone can open and explore.

    the research behind it

    my research started with a simple question: can satellite data show how wildfires and sulfur dioxide are connected to the air people breathe? i studied sulfur dioxide with sulfur scout, studied wildfires through fire hotspots and burn scars, and then put both on one globe so i can compare them for the same place and the same dates.

    my applications

    sulfur scout

    i use sulfur scout to follow sulfur dioxide from space. the layer reads sentinel-5p satellite data as a 7-day median, then compares any pinned spot with its own 30-day normal, so a volcano like kīlauea or an industrial plume stands out at a glance.

    wildfire detection

    for wildfires, i pair nasa firms hotspots with sentinel-2 burn scars. drop a pin and firescout counts fire pixels, measures burned hectares, and flags when fires and raised sulfur dioxide show up together, like during the los angeles fires of january 2025.
